If you spend eight hours a day at a desk, your feet probably aren't where you think about them. But they should be. A footrest isn't just a luxury—it's ergonomic furniture that actually changes how your entire body sits, your circulation flows, and how much fatigue you feel by 5 PM.

The right footrest keeps your feet flat and supported, reduces pressure on your thighs, improves blood circulation to your lower legs, and takes strain off your lower back. When your feet dangle or sit at awkward angles, you compensate by shifting your weight, tensing your core, and eventually developing postural issues that ripple up your spine. After testing dozens of models from budget picks to premium options, we've narrowed down the best footrests that actually deliver on comfort and durability.

## Humanscale Foot Machine

The Humanscale Foot Machine is a rocker-style footrest that does something different: it encourages subtle movement while you work. Instead of sitting statically on your feet, you rock forward and backward on a curved platform, engaging your calf muscles and keeping blood flowing.

**Key specs:** Curved rocker platform, 19.5" wide, works with any desk height, no assembly required, non-slip rubber base.

**Who it suits:** People who want active sitting and subtle movement throughout the day. Great for desk workers who fidget naturally or want to combat the "dead leg" feeling.

**Price:** $175–$225. [Check Price on Amazon](https://amazon.com?tag=homeoffice038-20)

The motion is genuinely subtle—you won't be doing squats at your desk—but it's enough to keep your legs from going numb. The issue is that it's pricey for a footrest, and it only works if you actively use the rocking motion. Some users report that the novelty wears off and they revert to static sitting.

## ErgoFoam Adjustable Footrest

This is the workhorse footrest. It's not fancy, but it does exactly what a footrest should do: adjust to keep your feet flat and supported at whatever desk height you're using.

**Key specs:** 6-level angle adjustment (0° to 30°), textured anti-slip surface, 17" wide platform, weighs 3.5 lbs, supports up to 330 lbs.

**Who it suits:** Anyone with a standard desk setup. Works for people of different heights and desk configurations. Solid choice for offices where you might need to adjust the angle throughout the day.

**Price:** $45–$65. [Check Price on Amazon](https://amazon.com?tag=homeoffice038-20)

We tested this for three months and it stayed exactly where we put it. The adjustability is genuinely useful—we found ourselves changing the angle depending on whether we were typing intensively or taking calls. The textured surface grips your shoes without being rough. At under $60, it's hard to beat for value.

## Upright Go Footrest (Companion Product)

If you're already using the Upright Go posture corrector, Upright's footrest is designed to work as part of a complete ergonomic system. It's a sleek, minimalist platform with a slight angle built in.

**Key specs:** 18" wide platform, 15° fixed angle, weighted base prevents sliding, modern design fits aesthetic desks, connects to the Upright ecosystem via app.

**Who it suits:** Upright Go users who want a matching, design-forward footrest. People who value brand cohesion and want ergonomic feedback integrated into their setup.

**Price:** $79–$99. [Check Price on Amazon](https://amazon.com?tag=homeoffice038-20)

The app integration is gimmicky—it mainly tells you whether your feet are actually on the rest—but the footrest itself is well-made and attractive. If you're not in the Upright ecosystem, there's no real advantage over the ErgoFoam. For those already invested, it's a logical addition.

## AmazonBasics Footrest with Massage Surface

This budget option takes a different approach: instead of pure support, it combines a footrest with a textured massage surface designed to stimulate pressure points on your feet and arch.

**Key specs:** 17" wide, fixed 15° angle, textured acupressure nodules, non-slip base, compact and lightweight at 2.8 lbs.

**Who it suits:** Budget-conscious buyers who also want mild foot massage benefits. People who like the acupressure mat trend and want something for their desk.

**Price:** $28–$40. [Check Price on Amazon](https://amazon.com?tag=homeoffice038-20)

Honestly, the massage aspect is nice but not transformative—the real benefit is that it's a decent footrest at a genuinely low price. The texture keeps your feet from sliding around, and at $30, it's a low-risk way to test whether a footrest actually improves your comfort. Our main complaint is that the fixed angle might not suit everyone.

## Fellowes Office Suites Footrest

Fellowes is known for ergonomic office products, and their footrest is a refined middle-ground option. It has gentle angle adjustment, a contoured surface, and industrial durability.

**Key specs:** 3-level height adjustment, textured contoured surface with arch support indentation, 18" wide, supports up to 300 lbs, 5-year warranty.

**Who it suits:** Office professionals who want a slightly premium footrest without the price tag of boutique brands. People who appreciate durability and warranty support.

**Price:** $55–$75. [Check Price on Amazon](https://amazon.com?tag=homeoffice038-20)

The contoured design with the arch indentation is genuinely thoughtful. It guides your foot into a naturally supportive position rather than just providing a flat platform. The warranty also matters if you're using this eight hours a day. It's not flashy, but it's solid.

## Yamazaki Home Steel Footrest

For the minimalist aesthetic office, Yamazaki makes a metal footrest that looks like actual furniture rather than ergonomic equipment. It's a steel frame with a horizontal bar positioned at foot height.

**Key specs:** Steel construction with powder-coated finish, 23" wide, 7" height, modern design, weighs 4.2 lbs, available in black or white.

**Who it suits:** People working in design-forward spaces who want their footrest to look intentional rather than functional. Works well under standing desks or in minimalist office setups.

**Price:** $60–$85. [Check Price on Amazon](https://amazon.com?tag=homeoffice038-20)

This is technically a footrest bar rather than a platform, so it works differently—your foot rests against the bar rather than on a surface. This reduces pressure more effectively for some people and creates more airflow around your feet. The design is genuinely attractive, which matters if your office is visible on video calls.

## Final Verdict

For most home office workers, the **ErgoFoam Adjustable Footrest** is the best choice. It's affordable, adjustable, durable, and solves the core problem—keeping your feet flat and your circulation flowing—without gimmicks or complexity. If budget is your primary concern, the AmazonBasics option delivers 85% of the ergonomic benefit at half the price.

If you want active movement and can justify the investment, the Humanscale Foot Machine is genuinely effective for fidgety workers. For aesthetics, the Yamazaki option actually looks like real furniture. And if you appreciate brand-cohesive ergonomic systems, the Upright option integrates well with their ecosystem.

The real takeaway: almost any footrest is better than no footrest. Your feet will thank you within a week of using one, and the accumulated reduction in strain over months and years is substantial. Start with one option, test it for a full week of work, and if it doesn't feel right, you'll know quickly. Most footrests have easy return policies—use that to your advantage.
